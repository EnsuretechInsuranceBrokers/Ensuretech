3

Sum insured rule (rater type)

Match this to how your policy is actually configured — it decides which columns get filled and which stay blank, same as the validation logic on upload.

Age-based raters compute sum insured from date of birth on the insurer's side — the Suminsured column is intentionally left blank; just make sure your age range (Section 2) sits inside your policy's configured bands.
Family construct raters compute sum insured from how many adults/children are in the family — the Suminsured column is left blank. Turn on Dependents below so families actually have a construct to rate.

Off: the simple rater chosen above behaves exactly as before. On: the choice above is ignored and sum insured (and, if you ask, premium) is looked up in the rate rows you define below, the way the legacy rater does it.

This subtype has no upload sheet in the legacy app: its rates are typed in a form. Do the same here.

No upload sheet for this premium type in the legacy app (a form). One line per sum insured: sum insured, premium.

4

Dependents

Off by default — each employee is just a "Self" row, like a flat/simple upload. Turn this on to also generate spouse, children and parents.

A given employee only ever gets parents or parents-in-law, never both — most policies reject mixing the two.

Optional fields

Employee Code, First/Last Name, Gender, DOB, DOJ, Email, Mobile Number, Suminsured, Relationship, Should Verify Email and Default Password are always filled — they're confirmed mandatory. Everything below is confirmed not mandatory: leave it off and the column stays genuinely blank (nothing to fail validation on); only turn it on where you actually want the value tested.
